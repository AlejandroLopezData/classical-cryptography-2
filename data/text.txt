The history of secret writing is almost as old as writing itself. People have always had messages that they did not want others to read, and so they looked for ways to hide the meaning of their words. Some of the first methods were very simple. A writer could change the order of the letters in a message, or he could replace each letter with a different one. These two ideas are still the base of many systems that are used today. A reader who does not know the secret has to look for patterns in the text, because every language leaves traces that are hard to remove. Some letters are used much more often than others, and certain groups of letters appear together again and again. When a message is only shuffled, the letters stay the same and only their places change, so the count of each letter still tells us a lot about the original words. When a message is substituted, every letter is replaced by another one, and the shape of the table of counts is lost. With enough text, a careful person can measure these differences and decide which method was used, long before trying to find the key. This is why the length of a message matters so much, since a short note does not contain enough letters to show a clear pattern.
